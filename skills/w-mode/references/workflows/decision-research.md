# Decision research workflow

**Entry:** a consequential strategic, competitive, product, or technical
decision that needs a durable recommendation from evidence. This is not a
synonym for deep or high-volume web search. Casual fact lookup stays outside
w-mode; product-behavior measurement uses product evaluation.

## Method

Use one proportional spine at every rigor level:

1. Frame the decision, owner, action, time horizon, reversibility, options, and
   exit predicate.
2. State a leading hypothesis and at least one credible alternative, including
   a null, status-quo, or insufficient-evidence position when useful.
3. Define discriminating predictions or record why none is available.
4. Gather and grade evidence with provenance, date, relevance, method,
   limitations, and support/contradict/neutral direction.
5. Perform a deliberate counterevidence and falsification pass.
6. Update confidence and identify the assumptions or signals that would switch
   the recommendation.
7. Stop when plausible counterevidence is unlikely to change the choice or the
   expected decision value of more research is lower than its cost.
8. Project the accepted final grounding revision into a decision brief.

Prefer primary evidence for decisive claims. Strong secondary evidence may
support discovery, context, comparison, and synthesis. When primary evidence
cannot be obtained, record the failed acquisition path and disclose the
dependence. Keep observed facts, assumptions, interpretations, judgments,
confidence, implications, and unknowns distinct.

## Rigor

`standard` is the default for decision-grade strategic or competitive work.
Use `light` for narrow, reversible decisions and `intensive` for high-stakes,
broad, contested, or difficult-to-reverse decisions. State and record the
choice. Do not invent a user-facing token, time, query, cost, or source budget.
A user stop preserves current state and uncertainty and cannot be relabeled as
decision-ready.

**Required artifacts:** an accepted revisioned `grounding` artifact with a
`research` envelope; one immutable approved `research-contract` containing the
decision frame, hypotheses and alternatives, evidence requirements, source
policy, falsification tests, rigor, stopping rule, and terminal predicate; an
artifact comparison binding to the current grounding revision; research-domain
receipts and reviews; and a current `research-outcome`. The human brief is a
derived view, never a second canonical verdict store.

**Transitions:** specialize the shared lifecycle as `FRAMED` decision and exit
predicate, `GROUNDED` hypotheses and evidence plan, `SPECIFIED` approved
research contract, `PLANNED` evidence/falsification/synthesis/review task graph,
`ASSIGNED` disjoint investigator ownership, `IMPLEMENTED` accepted corpus and
synthesis at an immutable grounding comparison, `VERIFIED` evidence coverage,
provenance, counterevidence, confidence, and stopping checks, `REVIEWED`
decision-quality review, and `DELIVERED` current `decision-ready` or
`inconclusive` research outcome plus derived brief.

**Allowed skips:** a lightweight run may omit a task graph with a recorded
reason. No rigor level may omit the decision frame, alternatives, provenance,
counterevidence accounting, confidence rationale, stopping rationale,
limitations, or remaining unknowns.

**Gates:** user approval of a structured/program research contract;
consequential option or scope choices; unavailable private or authoritative
evidence; research that would require external contact, purchase, publication,
or another unauthorized mutation; ambiguous active-run selection; and any
requested user stop. An inconclusive outcome is honest completion only when its
terminal rule is met, not a bypass for missing work.

**Evidence and review:** receipts use the `research` domain and bind both the
current research-contract digest and artifact comparison digest. Required axes
are `decision-relevance`, `evidence-quality`, `alternative-coverage`,
`counterevidence`, and `uncertainty-calibration`. Intensive rigor additionally
requires `independent-challenge` and `method-reproducibility`. Quantitative
evidence, intervals, or probabilities are welcome when justified; qualitative
confidence with rationale is the default.

**Terminal condition:** the exit predicate is true; every required criterion
has fresh research evidence; required reviews pass; the final grounding,
contract, predicate event, receipts, and reviews match the recorded outcome;
remaining unknowns, limitations, switching conditions, and stopping rationale
are explicit; and the outcome is `decision-ready` or `inconclusive`.

**Reply projection:** recommended action or honest inconclusion; confidence and
rationale; strongest supporting and contradicting evidence; alternatives;
switching conditions; remaining unknowns and limitations; stopping rationale;
record digests; and exact next action.
